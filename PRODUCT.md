# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Two overlapping crowds: college students and working professionals who follow and play in a private, multi-sport tournament (the BCL). They bet across six sports — cricket, football, table tennis, volleyball, pool, and basketball — on their own league's matches. Usage is mobile-first and happens in bursts: around and between live matches, on a phone, one-handed, often with friends nearby. Money is real but collected offline by admins, who top up in-app wallets; users then bet their balance and track P&L.

A second, smaller audience is the admins who run the tournament — creating matches and markets, settling results, and managing wallets — but the primary surface is built for the bettor.

## Product Purpose

PrimeStake is a private, multi-sport prediction game for a closed league, played entirely with in-game credits that have no cash value. It exists to make predicting your own tournament feel like a real, professional sportsbook rather than a throwaway side project. Success is engagement: people predict often, come back to check live markets, fantasy contests and standings, and spread their play across all six sports — not just cricket.

## Positioning

A genuinely professional-looking multi-sport sportsbook for a private league — pool-based (pari-mutuel) odds, live scores, and auto-settlement that hold up next to the real apps, built for a crowd that plays across every sport their tournament runs.

## Brand Personality

Credible, energetic, and clean. It should carry the live, data-forward density of a real sportsbook (Bet365 / DraftKings) with the modern, sleek darkness of Stake / Polymarket and the vibrant, mobile-first polish of Dream11 / MPL — without ever tipping into any of their excesses. Confident and legitimate: a user should trust it with real stakes and feel it takes the game seriously. Energy comes from live states, motion, and leaderboard competition, not from noise.

## Anti-references

- Sketchy or spammy gambling: no seedy neon-casino look, flashing jackpots, fake "you won!" urgency, or offshore-bookie vibe.
- Cheap or toy-like: no school-project or play-money-demo feel, no clip-art, no default-framework look.
- Cluttered or overwhelming: no wall of tiny numbers and blinking markets that can't be parsed at a glance.
- Generic AI SaaS: no interchangeable purple-gradient, rounded-card startup template.

## Design Principles

Earn trust through polish. This is play-money in mechanics but should look and behave like a real sportsbook; craft is how a private league app becomes credible.

Energy without noise. Drive engagement with live states, motion, and competition — never with flashing offers or fake urgency. The excitement is the real match, surfaced well.

Legible density. Show rich live markets like the big books do, but keep every screen parseable at a glance. Density is earned by hierarchy, not tolerated as clutter.

Thumb-first. Design for a phone held one-handed around a live match: reachable actions, generous tap targets, fast bet placement.

One system, every sport. Six sports share one coherent visual and interaction language so the app feels unified, not like six bolted-together mini-apps.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Mobile-first is the priority: large tap targets, one-handed reach, and layouts that hold up on small screens. On top of that, a WCAG AA baseline — readable contrast (especially the muted-blue text on dark navy, which needs checking), keyboard access, and reduced-motion support for all the live/animated states.
